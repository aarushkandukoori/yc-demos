A simplified model of the core idea behind a low-cost radar seeker: detect clutter on a noisy scope, designate a contact, and watch an onboard tracking loop hold lock through maneuvers until simulated intercept. Everything here runs client-side with synthetic targets — no real sensor data.

The real engineering problem a radar seeker solves is distinguishing one real, maneuvering target from clutter and decoys, then holding a stable track as geometry changes fast — all on a processor cheap enough to put on every interceptor. This demo caricatures that loop: a scoring function favors contacts with consistent radial motion, a simple proportional-navigation tracker keeps a reticle converging on the designated target, and lock confidence decays if the target jukes hard enough to fall outside the tracking gate.
