Purge vs. Sink

Two identical wafer-tool mini-environments sit in the same dirty fab. The left one fights airborne molecular contamination (AMC) by flushing it out with nitrogen. The right one destroys it in place with a catalytic reactor. Open the doors and watch what each approach costs you.

Model

Each chamber is a well-mixed volume V. Concentration C (ppb) obeys dC/dt = S_out + q_leak·(C_fab - C) - (Q_purge/V)·C - k·C. Purge is pure dilution: it needs several chamber volumes of N₂ to recover. The destruction term k is a first-order sink, so recovery time is 1/(k + Q/V) no matter what the fab does. Door events swap a large fraction of the air with fab air for the door duration. Wafer dose is the time integral of C; the film estimate assumes a fixed sticking coefficient. All parameters are invented for illustration and not calibrated to any real tool.
